Building AI environments to enhance human understanding of mathematics

The p-Nautilus project designs, tests and openly shares artificial intelligence (AI) environments that help researchers in mathematics learn, explore and communicate mathematics.

Browse the code

The project

Automated reasoning systems can now settle important mathematical questions with very little human intervention. The purpose of mathematics, though, is to advance human understanding (Thurston, 1994), and automation does not always serve it: a proof produced by a machine is not the same as a proof understood by a community.

Researchers have traditionally been formed by working through entry-level open problems, and large language models can now solve many of those on demand. We think new training methods can be built from dedicated AI environments and working protocols centred on communicating mathematical reasoning. The same environments could help experienced mathematicians enter technical topics in neighbouring disciplines and problems of wider social relevance.

Our aim is to deliver shareable AI environments, together with evidence of how well they work, gathered through methods from the social sciences.

AI environments

We borrow some keywords from AI engineering.

skill: A set of precise, reusable instructions that make a model behave in a specific, consistent way whenever a defined type of task appears.

artifact: The result a skill produces while completing a specific task. Artifacts may be intermediate or final, and are not necessarily reusable.

harness: A purpose-built operating environment that orchestrates an AI agent towards a task: a set of skills, the logic that coordinates them, and the connections to external tools, data sources and interfaces.

AI environment: A harness with a mathematically oriented purpose.

For example, a skill can guide a model through a first read of an unfamiliar paper; the reading map it produces is an artifact; a harness combines that skill with others and with the tools a team needs for a whole line of work.

Open source

Everything we build is published openly: skills, harnesses, connectors to external tools, protocols and documentation.
